The appellate phase begins the moment you are sentenced. A higher court reviews your case for legal errors — not to retry the facts, but to examine whether the law was applied correctly.

📌 Key DistinctionThe trial court decided what happened. The appellate court decides whether the law was followed. You are not retrying your case — you are pointing to specific legal mistakes.

The Chain of Review

  • Trial Court — where conviction and sentence happened
  • Florida District Court of Appeal (DCA) — first appellate stop, where most appeals are decided
  • Florida Supreme Court — discretionary only; does not take most cases
  • U.S. Supreme Court — certiorari only; extremely rare
⏱️ 30-Day Hard DeadlineYou have exactly 30 days from sentencing to file your Notice of Appeal. Courts almost never grant extensions. Missing this is almost always fatal to your direct appeal.

The Notice of Appeal is a short document filed with the trial court clerk. If your attorney doesn't file it — you can file it yourself. It doesn't need to be perfect; it just needs to say you're appealing.

If You Have No Attorney

  1. Go to the trial court clerk's office immediately
  2. Ask for the Notice of Appeal form, or write a simple letter titled "Notice of Appeal"
  3. State: your name, your case number, and that you appeal your conviction and sentence
  4. File it — get a stamped copy for yourself
  5. Request appointment of appellate counsel in writing on the same day
⏰ 30 DAYS — Notice of Appeal

The Mandate is the official document the appellate court issues after its final decision, sending the case back to the trial court. It is the moment your conviction becomes "final" — and the moment every post-conviction clock starts.

🚨 The Trap Most People Fall IntoBoth clocks run at the same time. You must file your 3.850 within the FIRST YEAR to pause the federal clock. If you wait until year 2, your federal rights may be gone forever. Use the Deadline Calculator in Tools.

How to Get Your Mandate Date

  • Contact the Clerk of the DCA that handled your appeal
  • Request a certified copy of the Mandate in writing
  • Keep this document — everything flows from this date
  • Missing the 30-day Notice of Appeal window — This is almost always unrecoverable. File the day of sentencing if possible.
  • Not preserving issues at trial — If your attorney didn't object, the DCA may refuse to review it. This is called procedural default.
  • Raising new facts on direct appeal — New evidence belongs in a 3.850, not a direct appeal. The DCA only reviews what happened at trial.
  • Waiting past the federal 1-year deadline to file anything — File your 3.850 before year one is up, no exceptions.
  • Filing in the wrong court — Direct appeals go to the DCA. Not the trial court. Not the Florida Supreme Court.
📋 Florida State Court

Rule 3.850
Post-Conviction Relief

Florida's primary vehicle for raising constitutional claims after conviction — including ineffective counsel, new evidence, and illegal sentences.

⚠ Exhaustion Required
Federal court requires state court to rule first

Before 3.850, post-conviction claims went straight to federal court as habeas petitions — there was no organized state process. Courts were flooded.

The U.S. Supreme Court's landmark Gideon v. Wainwright (1963) established the right to appointed counsel, creating an explosion of ineffective assistance of counsel claims. Courts needed a mechanism to route these through state court first.

Florida created Rule 3.850 as a structured state-court process — keeping claims in state court before reaching federal review. Over the last 15 years it has been tightened significantly with stricter procedure and clearer grounds for relief.

📌 Key Cases That Built 3.850Strickland v. Washington (1984) — created the two-prong test still used today for IAC claims. Brady v. Maryland (1963) — state must disclose evidence favorable to the defense.
⏰ 2 YEARS — Standard Deadline

Filed from the date your conviction becomes final (Mandate date, after all direct review is exhausted).

Exceptions — These Let You File After 2 Years

  • Newly Discovered Evidence — Evidence that did not exist or could not have been found with due diligence at trial. Must file within 2 years of discovering it. Must show it probably would have produced an acquittal.
  • New Constitutional Right — A right newly recognized by the U.S. or Florida Supreme Court that applies retroactively. Must file within 2 years of the decision.
  • Illegal Sentence (Rule 3.800a) — A sentence that exceeds what the law allows can be challenged at any time. No deadline.
  • Manifest Injustice / Fundamental Error — A narrow, fact-specific exception for egregious constitutional violations. Courts apply this rarely.
⚠ Courts Are Strict"I didn't know about the deadline" is NOT an exception. File early — do not test this.
  • Ineffective Assistance of Counsel (IAC) — Most common ground. Must meet both Strickland prongs: (1) attorney's performance was deficient AND (2) that deficiency changed the outcome. Both must be proven.
  • Newly Discovered Evidence — New evidence that probably would have produced an acquittal at trial.
  • Brady / Giglio Violations — Prosecution hid evidence favorable to you (Brady) or knowingly presented false testimony (Giglio).
  • Involuntary Plea — Your guilty plea was not knowing, intelligent, or voluntary — often due to bad advice from counsel.
  • Newly Recognized Constitutional Right — A retroactive right the Supreme Court recognized after your conviction.
  • Illegal Sentence — Sentence exceeds what law allows for the offense.
⚠ Cannot Use 3.850 To:Relitigate facts already decided at trial, or raise issues that were raised and decided on direct appeal.
  1. Confirm you are within the 2-year window — or that you have a valid exception
  2. Identify your specific ground(s) — Be precise. "I'm innocent" is not a 3.850 ground. IAC, Brady, illegal sentence — those are grounds.
  3. Gather supporting documents — Trial transcripts, prior court orders, any new evidence
  4. Draft your motion — Florida requires specific formatting. The motion must state the grounds, supporting facts, and the relief requested.
  5. File in the trial court that convicted you — Not the DCA. The original trial court.
  6. Serve a copy on the State Attorney's Office
  7. Keep a stamped/file-marked copy of everything
  8. Wait for the State's response — Usually 30–60 days. The court may summarily deny or set an evidentiary hearing.
  9. If denied — appeal to the DCA — You have 30 days from denial to appeal to the District Court of Appeal.
✓ After the DCA Rules on Your 3.850 AppealIf the DCA denies it — this is your moment to file Federal Habeas § 2254, provided the federal 1-year clock has not expired.
🏛️ Federal Civil Rights

42 U.S.C. § 1983
Civil Rights Claims

A federal law that lets you sue state officials who violated your constitutional rights. Powerful — but heavily restricted for incarcerated people.

Section 1983 was passed after the Civil War. It lets citizens sue government officials who violated their constitutional rights while acting in their official capacity. Think of § 1983 as the hammer — the constitutional violation is the nail.

Two Things You Must Prove

  1. A person acting "under color of state law" (a government officer, guard, official) violated your rights
  2. The right violated is guaranteed by the U.S. Constitution or federal law
⏰ 4 YEARS — Florida § 1983 Deadline
📌 Florida's Statute of LimitationsFlorida applies its 4-year general personal injury statute to § 1983 claims. Your clock starts from the date the violation occurred — not from your conviction date.
  • Excessive Force — Officers used unreasonable force during arrest or while incarcerated
  • Deliberate Indifference to Medical Needs — Officials knowingly ignored a serious medical condition (8th Amendment)
  • Unconstitutional Conditions of Confinement — Inhumane living conditions, denial of basic necessities
  • First Amendment Retaliation — Punishment for filing grievances, restrictions on religious practice or mail
  • Due Process Violations — Disciplinary proceedings without proper notice or fair hearing
  • Equal Protection — Discrimination based on race, religion, or other protected class
⚠ Most Common Mistake§ 1983 CANNOT challenge your conviction or get you out of prison. That is what habeas corpus is for. Using § 1983 for this will get your case dismissed immediately.
  • Cannot challenge your conviction or sentence — Use habeas (§ 2254) for that
  • Cannot sue the State of Florida itself — 11th Amendment immunity. You sue the individual officer.
  • Cannot sue federal officials — § 1983 covers state actors only. Federal officers require a Bivens claim (a separate and much harder path)
  • Cannot get money from officials in "official capacity" — You can get damages from them personally, not in their official role

The Prison Litigation Reform Act (PLRA), passed in 1996, was sold as reducing "frivolous" lawsuits. In reality it creates enormous barriers for even legitimate claims.

PLRA Requirements You Cannot Ignore

  • Exhaustion of Administrative Remedies — You MUST complete every level of the prison grievance process before filing in court. Skip one step — even if the process is futile or unfair — and the court will dismiss your case. No exceptions.
  • Three Strikes Rule — If 3 prior lawsuits were dismissed as frivolous, malicious, or for failure to state a claim, you cannot file for free (in forma pauperis) in the future — unless you are in "imminent danger of serious physical injury."
  • Physical Injury Requirement — For most damages claims, you must show actual physical injury. Emotional distress alone is rarely enough.
  • Filing Fees — Even with IFP status, you still owe the full filing fee — it's collected over time from your prison account.
🚨 Exhaustion Is the #1 KillerDocument EVERY grievance. Keep copies of every submission AND every response. Complete ALL levels before you file anything in court. This single requirement defeats more valid claims than anything else.
🗝️ Federal Court

Federal Habeas
28 U.S.C. § 2254

The last resort for state prisoners to challenge their conviction in federal court. The rules are strict, the deadlines are absolute, and the traps are everywhere.

The Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA) imposed a strict 1-year statute of limitations on federal habeas petitions. Before 1996, there was no deadline.

⏰ 1 YEAR — AEDPA Federal Deadline

When Does the 1 Year Begin?

The clock starts from the latest of these four dates:

  1. The date your conviction becomes final on direct review (Mandate date)
  2. The date a state-created barrier to filing is removed
  3. The date the U.S. Supreme Court recognized a new constitutional right that applies retroactively
  4. The date you discovered (or reasonably could have discovered) the factual basis of your claim

Tolling — What Pauses the Federal Clock

A properly filed state post-conviction motion (like 3.850) pauses the federal clock while it is pending in state court.

🚨 Two Critical Warnings on Tolling1. The state motion must be filed BEFORE the 1-year federal clock expires. A state filing cannot revive a dead clock.

2. The motion must be "properly filed." If the state court rejects it as untimely, it does NOT toll the federal deadline.

Before a federal court hears your habeas petition, you must have exhausted your state remedies — raised every claim through the state courts all the way to the highest available state court.

What Exhaustion Requires

  • You raised the claim on direct appeal AND/OR in a 3.850 motion
  • You appealed the denial of your 3.850 all the way through the DCA
  • The state courts had a full and fair opportunity to rule on the constitutional issue
⚠ Procedural Default TrapIf you raise a claim in federal court that you never raised in state court, the federal court will dismiss it — or worse, find it "procedurally defaulted," which can permanently bar the claim even if you go back to state court.
  • Violation of Federal Constitutional Rights — Due process, equal protection, 4th/5th/6th/8th Amendment violations
  • IAC not raised in state court — In limited circumstances under Martinez v. Ryan (2012)
  • Actual Innocence — A narrow gateway to get around procedural barriers if new evidence shows no reasonable juror would have convicted you
  • Brady Violations not discovered before — Prosecution withheld evidence that would have changed the outcome
📌 The AEDPA Standard of ReviewFederal courts do not simply re-examine your case. They ask: was the state court's decision "contrary to" or an "unreasonable application of" clearly established federal law? This is a very high bar — deliberately so.

Pre-Filing Checklists

📋 Before You File a Rule 3.850

0 / 8
  • I have a copy of my Mandate (or know the exact date my conviction became final)
  • I am within the 2-year deadline — or I have a valid legal exception
  • My claim was NOT already raised on direct appeal
  • I have identified a specific constitutional ground (IAC, Brady, new evidence — not just "I'm innocent")
  • If claiming IAC — I can show both deficient performance AND that it changed the outcome (both Strickland prongs)
  • I am filing in the correct court — the trial court that sentenced me (not the DCA)
  • My motion meets Florida's formatting requirements
  • I have kept a stamped copy of everything I filed

🏛️ Before You File a § 1983 Claim

0 / 7
  • I have completed ALL levels of the prison grievance process — no steps skipped
  • I have copies of every grievance filed and every response received
  • I am within the 4-year Florida statute of limitations from the date of the violation
  • My claim involves a STATE official (not a federal officer)
  • I am NOT trying to challenge my conviction — that requires habeas, not § 1983
  • I have identified the specific constitutional right that was violated
  • I have checked whether I have 3 prior strikes under the PLRA
